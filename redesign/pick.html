Eight directions

All eight answer the same question the old home screen didn’t: what can I cook right now. Each one changes the layout, not just the colours. Scroll inside a phone to see more of it. Nothing here touches your saved kitchen.

A

Service

A working kitchen’s pass. Dishes come up as printed tickets, torn edge and all, most-ready first, with a monospace count of what you have. The most distinctive — and the biggest gamble.

Open A full size
B

Larder

Editorial. One dish is put forward for tonight and given the whole top of the screen; the rest waits below as a quiet index. Porcelain, ink and a serif. The closest to a premium cooking app.

Open B full size
C

Readiness

A tool. Every dish is a row with a meter, one segment per ingredient, so “one away” is something you can count. Eleven dishes fit where the old list showed two.

Open C full size
E

Épicerie

The colours of a French grocer’s shelf — cobalt, tomato, butter yellow on warm white. Each dish wears its cuisine as a coloured spine, so you learn the list the way you learn a supermarket aisle.

Open E full size
F

Spice

Every colour lifted from the spice rack: turmeric, paprika, sumac, cumin, pistachio. Heat is the organising idea — the screen warms up as you scroll toward the fierce end. Your “kick”, made visible.

Open F full size
G

After Dark

Deep indigo with one electric lime that only ever means “you can cook this”. Leads with the single number that is the whole answer. The most modern — and the most like a paid app.

Open G full size
H

Shelves

Colour with a job. Fridge is blue, freezer ice, pantry wheat, spices rust — everywhere, forever. Beads on each dish show which shelves you’ll open, and a hollow bead is the one letting you down.

Open H full size
D

Tonight

No list at all. One dish, full screen, swipe for the next. Everything is thumb-sized. Boldest, and it lives or dies on the photo — a dark one leaves the top of the screen empty, as here.
